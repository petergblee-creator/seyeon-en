Korea's 19% flat tax for foreign employees: should you elect it?

Peter Lee (Lee Gyubum), CPA licensed in Korea & US · November 2026
In short: Foreign employees in Korea can choose a flat 19% income tax (20.9% with local tax) on their Korean employment income instead of the normal 6–45% progressive rates. It usually pays off at higher salaries. The government has proposed raising the rate to 21% from 2027 income, so this year's choice and next year's numbers may differ.

How the flat tax option works

  • Who: foreign nationals receiving employment income from work performed in Korea.
  • Period: available for tax years ending within 20 years from the first day you started working in Korea.
  • Rate: 19% of gross employment income, plus 10% local income tax (20.9% in total).
  • The trade-off: if you elect the flat rate, you give up all exemptions, deductions and tax credits. Non-taxable allowances become part of the tax base.
  • How to elect: through your employer's monthly withholding or year-end settlement, or in your own annual income tax return (May).

As a rough guide, the flat rate starts to win at around KRW 140 million of salary at 19%, and around KRW 150 million if the rate rises to 21%. Below that, the progressive system with its deductions is usually cheaper.

Why this matters for US assignees

  • Foreign tax credit: US citizens are taxed by the US on worldwide income. Paying less Korean tax can mean a smaller foreign tax credit at home, so the real saving depends on both returns together.
  • Tax equalization: if your employer equalizes your taxes, the saving belongs to the company. HR and payroll should agree on the method before the first payslip.
  • Allowances: housing, school and relocation allowances are often large for assignees. Under the flat rate they are taxed too, which can shift the result.

What is changing

The 2026 tax reform proposal raises the flat rate from 19% to 21% for income arising from 1 January 2027 and extends the scheme, which was due to end on 31 December 2026, to 31 December 2029. As of November 2026 this is still a proposal awaiting the National Assembly. We will update this page once the law is final.

What to do now

  1. Run both methods on your expected 2026 and 2027 pay, including allowances.
  2. Check how the result interacts with your home-country return.
  3. Tell payroll which method to use before the next withholding cycle or year-end settlement.
